# Call 1 — Discovery

**Deal:** Cadenza (DialBridge) → Brighthaven Solar
**Speakers:** Rep = Rohan Mehta (Cadenza AE) · Prospect = Dana Cortez (Head of Sales)
**Stage:** Discovery

---

Rep: Hey Dana, can you hear me okay? Sometimes this thing takes a second.

Prospect: Yeah, I've got you. Loud and clear.

Rep: Perfect. Thanks for making the time. I know Fridays are a little sacred. Are we expecting anyone else on your side, or is it just the two of us today?

Prospect: Just me for now. If it goes anywhere I'll pull in our ops person, but I wanted to kick the tires myself first.

Rep: Totally fair, and honestly that's my favorite way to start. So this is the first real conversation between Brighthaven and Cadenza, right? I don't want to assume you know anything about us or waste your time pitching stuff you don't care about. Can I get the lay of the land from you first, and then I'll point at whatever's actually relevant?

Prospect: Sure. That works.

Rep: Great. So just at a high level, what pulled you into looking at a calling tool right now? Like why now, versus six months ago or six months from now?

Prospect: Honestly it's growth catching up with us. We've got twelve people on the inside-sales side calling homeowners all day, and right now they're basically using their own cell phones. It's a mess. Reps churn, they walk out the door with the phone that has all the customer texts on it, and I have zero visibility into what's actually being said on those calls.

Rep: Yeah. So no recording, no way to coach.

Prospect: None. If a deal goes sideways I'm reconstructing it from memory. And we had one rep last quarter who, let's just say, was promising things on calls that we do not offer. I found out from the customer.

Rep: Oof. That's the nightmare, right? You can't fix what you can't hear.

Prospect: Exactly.

Rep: Okay, that's super helpful. Let me poke at the volume side. Twelve reps — roughly how many dials is each one trying to get through in a day? Ballpark is fine.

Prospect: I'd love it to be a hundred. Realistically it's probably sixty, seventy? And a big chunk of that is just... the physical act of dialing. Punching numbers, waiting, getting a voicemail, hanging up, going again.

Rep: Right, so the tool is basically stealing productivity from them one dial at a time. That's the exact thing a parallel dialer is built to kill — I'll show you the mechanics on our next call rather than hand-wave it, but the short version is your rep talks to more live humans per hour and stops babysitting voicemails. Voicemail drop, local numbers so the homeowner actually picks up, all of that.

Prospect: The local number thing matters a lot for us. Solar's got a bit of a spam reputation, so a 1-800 or an out-of-state area code just gets ignored.

Rep: Yeah, local presence is going to be a big lever for you. We can make sure the number that shows up matches the region the homeowner's actually in. Okay — and where does this data need to live? Are you on a CRM today?

Prospect: HubSpot. And that's non-negotiable, honestly. If the calls don't log to HubSpot automatically, I don't want it. I'm not asking reps to copy-paste anything.

Rep: Good, that's music to my ears because our HubSpot sync is native — call logs, recordings, the AI summary of the call, it all lands on the contact record without anyone lifting a finger. Noted as a hard requirement, though. Let me write that down.

Prospect: Please do.

Rep: So let me make sure I've got the picture. Twelve seats, outbound to homeowners, HubSpot is the system of record, and the pains are: no visibility slash coaching, reps walking off with customer relationships, and raw dialing speed. Anything I'm missing?

Prospect: That's most of it. The other thing, quietly, is I want to look a little more grown-up. When we bring on an enterprise partner or an investor asks how the sales floor runs, "everyone uses their personal cell" is not a great answer.

Rep: Yeah, that's a real one. Credibility. Okay.
